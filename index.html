01 The Clinician

Pain is not always a measure of damage. It is the nervous system's best guess about threat — and my work is helping people, patients and students rewrite that guess.

Associate Professor and Head of Musculoskeletal Physiotherapy at MGM School of Physiotherapy (MGM Institute of Health Sciences, NAAC A++), with more than a decade of clinical work across Mumbai, Pune and Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar.

Trained at SVNIRTAR, Cuttack — one of India's national rehabilitation institutes — he blends manual therapy, graded loading and pain neuroscience education into a biopsychosocial approach that treats the person, not just the tissue.

An active member of the International Association for the Study of Pain (IASP), he researches, publishes and teaches the next generation of physiotherapists to think like clinicians — not protocol followers.

      03 Philosophy · The Pain Pathway
      01 — Tissue

      It starts with a signal, not with pain.

      Nociceptors in tissue detect potential threat — load, heat, chemistry. They send danger signals, not pain.

      02 — Spinal cord

      The signal is filtered.

      The spinal cord amplifies or dampens the message. In chronic pain, this gate can stay "turned up" — central sensitisation.

      03 — Brain

      The brain weighs the evidence.

      Beliefs, fear, past experience, sleep, stress and context all vote on one question: how dangerous is this?

      04 — Output

      Pain is the brain's output.

      Pain protects — but it can persist long after tissues heal. That is why education and graded movement work.

      05 — The Person

      Treat the person, not the scan.

      Biological, psychological and social factors converge. Good rehabilitation addresses all three.

      07 Clinical Insights

      Notes from the clinic.

      Short, evidence-informed lessons he shares with patients, students and clinicians. Drag to explore →

      Pain Science

      Pain doesn't always mean damage

      Beliefs, fear and perceived threat shape pain. Education recalibrates threat — so movement can return with confidence.

      Clinical Reasoning

      Rehab fails on reasoning, not exercise

      Was the assessment enough? Was the load right? Can the decision be explained? That separates routine from professional practice.

      Shoulder

      Rotator cuff: a capacity problem

      Overload, scapular control and overhead demand — rehab rebuilds capacity rather than prescribing rest.

      Hand & Wrist

      Carpal tunnel, recognised early

      Night symptoms and thumb-to-ring-finger tingling are key clues. Neural mobility and ergonomics change the course.

      Elbow

      Tennis elbow is about load

      Progressive (eccentric) loading, motor control and activity modification restore tendon capacity.

      Foot

      Plantar fasciitis: more than heel pain

      Load modification, calf–fascia mobility and intrinsic foot strength prevent recurrence.

      Digital Health

      Tech-neck

      Every inch of forward head posture adds load. Eye-level screens and 30-minute breaks go a long way.

      Workplace

      The 60-second reset

      Micro-breaks re-activate posture muscles, reset tension and recharge focus — a minute you shouldn't skip.

      Recovery

      Sleep, the forgotten therapy

      Deep sleep repairs tissue and regulates pain sensitivity. Patients who sleep well heal better.

      Active Ageing

      You grow old when you stop moving

      Movement, purpose and connection protect strength, balance and memory.

      Mind–Body

      Your body hears your mind

      Stress and fear amplify pain signals. When the patient feels heard, healing begins.

      ◌ Take 30 seconds

      Pain eases when you breathe.

      Ready30

      Slow breathing calms the nervous system and turns down the body's pain alarm. Breathe in for 4, out for 6.
